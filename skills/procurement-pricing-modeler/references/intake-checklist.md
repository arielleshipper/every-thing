# Procurement Intake Checklist

Use this checklist to ask the user for pointers before searching or modeling. The goal is to find the economic terms, actual usage, and decision context quickly.

## Business Context

- What tool or vendor category is being evaluated?
- Who is the incumbent vendor?
- Which challenger vendors are in scope?
- Is there another vendor that can do the same job similarly or better?
- What business problem is the tool solving?
- Is the goal cost reduction, better terms, consolidation, feature improvement, risk reduction, or vendor leverage?

## Source Pointers

- Which emails, Slack threads, Notion pages, call transcripts, or meeting notes should be reviewed?
- Is there a recent invoice showing actual spend and usage?
- Is there an order form, contract, renewal notice, proposal, or vendor deck?
- Is the product self-serve with a public pricing page?
- Are there click-through terms that matter for commitment, cancellation, usage limits, or renewal?
- Has the vendor given projections or usage recommendations? Where?

## Usage And Forecast

- Current usage by billing unit: seats, contacts, sends, API calls, storage, events, revenue, or other units.
- Historical usage over the last 3, 6, and 12 months when available.
- Forecast range: conservative, expected, aggressive.
- Internal forecast and vendor forecast, with differences called out explicitly.
- Growth shape: immediate step-up, linear ramp, compound monthly growth, seasonality, or lumpy campaign-driven usage.
- Active vs stored units when relevant, especially contacts, seats, workspaces, or records.

## Pricing Terms

- Base price and included usage.
- Overage rate and overage billing unit.
- Annual commitment, monthly billing, or prepaid credits.
- Minimum spend, true-up, ramp deal, rollover, refund, or cancellation terms.
- Discount expiration and renewal uplift.
- Feature differences between tiers.
- Support, onboarding, implementation, and data migration costs.
- Public pricing page terms for self-serve products.
- Invoice details for incumbent spend, including actual usage, discounts, taxes, and one-time fees.

## Decision Risks

- Underutilization risk: what happens if usage is below commit?
- Overutilization risk: what happens if usage exceeds commit?
- Migration cost: engineering time, data migration, workflow disruption, contract overlap.
- Vendor lock-in: annual term, proprietary data, integration depth, deliverability/reputation, admin burden.
- Operational fit: whether the billing primitive matches how the company actually uses the product.
- Implementation scope: engineering work, admin setup, security review, migration, training, and operational owner.

## Vendor Questions

- What exactly counts as a billable unit?
- Are inactive/stored units billed differently from active units?
- What happens if we underuse the committed volume?
- What happens if we exceed the committed volume?
- Can the commitment ramp up over time instead of starting at the target volume immediately?
- Can pricing be monthly while preserving the discounted rate?
- What is the best price at each usage scenario in our forecast range?
- Are there implementation, support, deliverability, data retention, or compliance costs not shown in the quote?
