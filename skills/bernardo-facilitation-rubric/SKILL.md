# Facilitated Session Rubric — "The Bernardo Method"

A reusable checklist for designing and reviewing facilitated workshops (sprints, goal-setting, brainstorms). Derived from three FigJam boards Bernardo ran at Donut: an AI Brainstorm (1:1s), a Roadmap Sprint, and a 2026 Goals session.

## How to use this file

**For designing a session:** walk the phases top to bottom and make sure each element exists on your board *before* the session starts.

**For reviewing a session (agent instructions):** you will be given a workshop board (image, export, or description). For each checklist item below, mark `✅ present`, `⚠️ partial`, or `❌ missing`, quote the on-board evidence, and give a one-line fix for anything not fully present. Then produce the scorecard at the bottom. Be specific and concrete; do not give generic advice.

---

## Core principle: "messy middle, clean edge"

Bernardo's boards separate two spaces. The **center** is generative and messy — sticky storms, clusters, votes. The **edge** holds a **polished final artifact** that the raw material feeds into. Divergent mess and convergent output never occupy the same zone. A well-run board can be read left-to-right (or center-out) as a story: prompt → raw ideas → clustered ideas → voted winners → written artifact.

## Phase 1 — Setup (built before anyone arrives)

- [ ] **Titled and dated.** Board has a clear title + date (e.g. "AI brainstorm / 1:1s / Sept 9th 2025").
- [ ] **Sectioned.** Distinct labeled sections/frames ("Section 1"), not one undifferentiated canvas.
- [ ] **Pre-built scaffolding.** Empty sticky grids with placeholder text ("Add text") are laid out in advance so participants drop into structure, not a blank page.
- [ ] **Color-coded with a legend.** Sticky/zone colors carry meaning and there's a visible key (e.g. grey = to-do, yellow = in progress, green = done).
- [ ] **Low activation energy.** A participant can tell where to write within seconds of landing.

## Phase 2 — Framing

- [ ] **Tiny prompts, not briefings.** Each activity is anchored by a single short question ("Where do we want to be in 3 months?", "What can prevent us from getting there?", "How might we overcome our challenges?"). No paragraph of instructions on the canvas.
- [ ] **Verb-driven prompts.** Prompts point at a decision or output, not a discussion topic.
- [ ] *(Optional / often off-board)* **Warm-up / icebreaker.** Note: none of the three source boards had an on-canvas icebreaker — they ran task-first. If a warm-up matters, run it verbally or on a separate board. The nearest on-board substitutes are the silent-writing grid (gentle entry) and playful emoji reactions (light tone).

## Phase 3 — Diverge (generate)

- [ ] **Silent sticky storm.** A wall/grid of stickies filled independently before discussion, to avoid anchoring/groupthink.
- [ ] **Individual contribution visible.** Optional per-person lane ("Names" column) so everyone demonstrably contributes.
- [ ] **Anonymity where it helps.** Idea stickies in challenge/opportunity clusters are un-attributed to reduce politics during generation.
- [ ] **"How might we…" reframes.** Problems are converted into solvable question form.

## Phase 4 — Converge (prioritize)

- [ ] **Affinity clustering.** Raw stickies grouped into labeled, color-zoned buckets with named categories.
- [ ] **Lightweight voting.** Dot / emoji voting (⭐ 🔥 ❤️ "+1") to surface signal — not a heavy weighted rubric (rigor is reserved for strategy boards, see Phase 6).
- [ ] **Votes preserved downstream.** Vote counts travel into the synthesis ("5 votes: …", "3 votes: …") rather than being discarded.
- [ ] **Winners promoted into a distinct container.** Top ideas pulled into a titled frame (e.g. "Top goals" 👑).
- [ ] **Traceable lineage.** Connector lines link promoted winners back to their source stickies.

## Phase 5 — Synthesize into a final artifact

- [ ] **Dedicated output container** at the edge of the board, visually distinct from the working area.
- [ ] **Re-grouped by theme.** Raw notes reorganized into themed sections ("Organized by Theme"), not left as a pile.
- [ ] **Board language → document language.** The artifact is written in clean prose/structure you could paste into a doc and ship.
- [ ] **Repeating micro-structure.** Each item follows a consistent template (e.g. goals broken into *The Cadence / The Focus / Working on*).
- [ ] **Mapped to workstreams.** Each synthesized goal/theme links to concrete workstreams or next steps.

## Phase 6 — Sharpen the thinking (strategy / roadmap boards)

- [ ] **Falsifiable, measurable outcomes.** Future-state statements are specific and gradeable ("50 teams using it weekly," "ship 5–8 use cases").
- [ ] **Kill criteria.** Bets include an explicit "or we kill it / merge it" condition.
- [ ] **Pre-mortem run.** A dedicated "what could prevent us / what breaks this" pass, sequenced *after* the optimistic vision (kept separate from it).
- [ ] **Keep / stop pass.** An explicit "keep doing 👍 / stop doing 👎" pruning step.
- [ ] **Hypothesis mad-lib.** Fill-in-the-blank: "If we help ___ / solve ___ / with ___ / they'll choose us over ___ / because ___."
- [ ] **Competitive framing.** "800 lb gorilla" vs. other competitors; Advantage split into Capability and "what will change vs. won't."
- [ ] **Roadmap grid (if roadmapping).** Streams × time-cycles, a DRI avatar per lane, status colors, effort/t-shirt sizing, and "Now" clearly flagged (👉).

## Known absences (by design — don't over-correct)

These were intentionally light or missing in the source boards; treat their absence as normal unless the session's purpose demands otherwise:

- No walls of written instructions (prompts stay one line).
- No timeboxes / agenda clock on the board itself (sequence is implied by layout).
- No pre-read or data appendix on the canvas.
- No named attribution on most idea stickies.
- Brainstorm boards don't end in a who/what/by-when action list — they hand off to a separate roadmap artifact that carries owners. (If a brainstorm is meant to be terminal, *add* an owners+dates step.)
- No heavy scoring rubric on brainstorms — voting is deliberately lightweight.

**The three make-or-break signals** (call these out explicitly):
1. Is there a **pre-built scaffold**, or a blank canvas?
2. Does the board **converge** (cluster → vote → promote), or just accumulate stickies?
3. Is there a **clean final artifact** in document language, separate from the mess?

**One-paragraph verdict:** what most aligns with the Bernardo method, the single highest-leverage gap, and whether the board tells a legible start-to-finish story.
