# Social — LinkedIn Post

Writes LinkedIn posts for product launches, thought leadership, and brand building.

## When to invoke

- When drafting a LinkedIn post for a launch, announcement, or brand moment
- When a launch skill orchestrates channel execution and needs the LinkedIn deliverable
- When writing standalone LinkedIn content for a brand or individual

## Dependencies

- `foundation/marketing-os`
- `brand-voice/{relevant brand}` (loaded dynamically)
- `positioning/{relevant brand}` (loaded dynamically)
- `craft/copywriting`
- `craft/editing`

Brand is resolved dynamically based on what invokes this skill.

## Channel-specific notes

- Hook in the first 1–2 lines before the "see more" fold
- Professional context but human tone — avoid LinkedIn-voice clichés
- 1,300-character sweet spot for engagement; 3,000-character max

## Arielle's thought-leadership posts

Lead with a specific operating tension Arielle has actually encountered, including what she knows and what remains uncertain. Let that earn the broader thesis. Test every reference and transition as if the reader knows neither the previous draft nor Every.

Make an abstract prediction concrete with familiar work or budget examples. Show a current experiment as a step toward the thesis, not as proof that the whole future has arrived. Keep the post to the examples needed for this argument.

Use plain, precise language and short paragraphs. Let any humor arise from the subject and lead naturally to a genuine invitation to talk; don't attach a clever closing line that could fit any post.

## Publication handoff and learning

Before handing off a post, account for its full publishing package: the post, any image, and any source or event links. If links belong in a first comment, draft that comment with clear labels so Arielle can publish without a second writing pass.

When Arielle says a post is ready or live, ask once for the end state: the final text or post URL, any companion comment, and what she changed or rejected. If the live post is accessible, inspect it. Never assume the last draft was published unchanged.

Compare that end state with the draft and Arielle's feedback. Run the `self-improve` workflow to identify durable LinkedIn-writing lessons. Propose the exact skill change and get Arielle's approval before editing. If there is no reusable lesson, say so rather than adding a rule for its own sake.
