# Commission Plan Review

Use this skill for first-pass review of commission or incentive compensation documents. Treat the work as both incentive design and legal/ops risk review. The goal is not to give final legal advice; it is to spot what the plan actually promises, where someone could later claim unintended commission, and what should be clarified before the document goes to counsel or signature.

## Core Posture

- Read the source documents before forming a view: plan, side letter, offer letter, SOW, order form language, comp plan, comments, and any business context.
- Ask what behavior the company wants to incentivize, then test whether the words do that and only that.
- Be company-protective, operationally realistic, and scenario-driven.
- Separate legal risk, business judgment, and operational burden.
- Prefer clear definitions and examples over broad entitlement language.
- Do not over-lawyer small issues, but do flag language that creates an accidental pay obligation.
- If legal conclusions matter, frame them as issues for counsel rather than final legal advice.

## Review Workflow

1. Identify the document architecture.
   - Name each controlling document: offer letter, exhibit, SIP, side letter, SOW, commission plan, plan acknowledgement, or amendment.
   - Check whether the commission language belongs inside an offer letter exhibit or standalone plan.
   - Look for supersession, no-duplicate-commission, amendment, at-will, and no-guarantee language.

2. State the business intent in plain English.
   - Who is being paid?
   - What behavior or revenue are we trying to reward?
   - Is the plan for new sales, business development partnerships, customer success expansion, renewals, product usage, consulting, or something mixed?
   - Does the person's base comp change how generous the commission should be?

3. Map the money.
   - Define commissionable revenue.
   - Define excluded revenue.
   - Identify the data source for calculation: signed agreement, invoice, payment record, ARR system, CRM, finance records, or company accounting.
   - Check whether commission is based on booked revenue, ARR, cash received, cleared funds, recognized revenue, new ARR, incremental ARR, or another metric.

4. Test credit and contribution.
   - Ask whether the employee can earn commission on deals sourced by others.
   - Ask whether minimal involvement, routing paperwork, or sending an agreement could create a claim.
   - Look for credit splitting, management discretion, account assignment, limited-involvement adjustments, and final interpretive authority.

5. Test timing and operations.
   - Distinguish when commission is earned from when it is paid.
   - Prefer monthly processing where practical, such as payment within 30 days after the close of the prior month for fully executed deals for which cleared funds have been received.
   - Avoid language that forces one-off commission processing each time a payment lands unless the business explicitly wants that overhead.
   - Check tax withholding, standard payroll deductions, reconciliation, and correction of overpayments or underpayments.

6. Test lifecycle and eligibility.
   - Check plan period, start date, participant eligibility, acknowledgement requirement, role changes, leave, disability, death, termination, and post-employment payments.
   - Check whether the company has a defined post-employment payout window, such as 90 days for qualifying funds received after employment ends.
   - Check whether revenue from agreements signed after employment ends is excluded unless expressly agreed in writing.

7. Test edge cases before drafting comments.
   - Bundled deal with both commissionable and non-commissionable components.
   - Consulting-led deal where another person sells product usage and the plan participant only handles paperwork.
   - Product subscription, team subscription, or usage-based product revenue that could look like a partnership.
   - Flat renewal vs expansion or upsell.
   - Customer converts from self-serve to enterprise with no incremental revenue.
   - Refund, credit, cancellation, chargeback, nonpayment, or bad debt.
   - Multiple employees claim credit for the same deal.
   - Deal signed before termination but paid after termination.
   - Contractor SOW commission overlaps with employee commission plan.

## Arielle's Commission Plan Principles

- Commission should usually reward new or incremental revenue, not flat renewals, unless the company intentionally chooses otherwise.
- Product purchases, ordinary subscriptions, team subscriptions, usage-based products, and consulting revenue should be excluded unless expressly approved for a specific deal.
- Bundled deals need allocation language and company discretion.
- Someone should not be able to claim commission merely because a deal passed through their function.
- Monthly payout cadence is often more operationally sane than paying after each payment receipt.
- Cash receipt matters, but payout should still allow time for month-end close, reconciliation, and payroll.
- The company should be able to offset or adjust commission for refunds, credits, cancellations, chargebacks, nonpayment, and overpayments.
- Plan language should preserve final company authority to interpret, administer, split credit, adjust assignments, and change future plan terms.
- Side letters and exhibits should not conflict with offer letters, SIPs, SOWs, or existing comp plans.

## Common Red Flags

- "Regardless of who sourced or introduced the opportunity" without meaningful contribution or credit rules.
- "Managed through business development" without saying what level of involvement is required.
- Commission paid within 30 days after each payment receipt, creating rolling operational overhead.
- Renewals, extensions, and expansions grouped together when only new or incremental revenue should be paid.
- Product subscriptions or usage-based products not clearly excluded from partnership revenue.
- "Single purchase" language that conflicts with usage-based or team subscription scenarios.
- No allocation method for bundled deals.
- No company discretion for credit splitting or limited involvement.
- No clawback, offset, or adjustment language.
- No post-employment timing rule.
- No no-duplicate-commission language across SOW and employment plan.
- No plan amendment, termination, final interpretation, confidentiality, or no-employment-guarantee language.

## Commenting Style

When drafting comments, sound like Arielle: direct, practical, and scenario-based. Lead with the business implication, then ask whether the team is comfortable with that, then suggest the cleaner route.

Good comment patterns:

- "As written, this could let [person] claim commission on deals other people bring in if he has very minimal involvement. Are we comfortable with that?"
- "This seems to require processing commissions as payments come in, which is a lot of operational overhead. I would strongly prefer monthly payout language."
- "I would be careful here. Flat renewals could become eligible for another commission. Given the base comp, I would default to new/incremental revenue only."
- "This carveout probably needs to cover team subscriptions and usage-based product revenue too, otherwise this gets murky in mixed consulting/product deals."
- "I think this should live as Exhibit A to the offer letter so the document architecture is clean."

## Safer Language Patterns

Use these as starting points, not final legal drafting.

Payment timing:

```text
Commission payments will be paid within 30 days after the close of the previous month for deals that are fully executed and for which cleared funds have been received by the Company, following the Company's reconciliation of the applicable signed agreement, invoice, payment record, and any applicable exclusions or adjustments.
```

New or incremental revenue:

```text
Commissionable revenue includes only new or incremental revenue above the customer's existing spend as determined by the Company in good faith based on its records as of the date the applicable agreement is fully executed.
```

Limited involvement:

```text
If the participant has no, limited, administrative, or shared involvement in obtaining a deal, the Company may reduce, split, or deny commission credit in its reasonable discretion.
```

Bundled deals:

```text
If a deal includes both commissionable and non-commissionable components, the Company will allocate revenue between those components in good faith based on the signed agreement, invoice structure, internal revenue treatment, and substance of the transaction. The Company will have final authority to determine the allocation for commission purposes.
```

Product and consulting exclusions:

```text
Commissionable revenue excludes consulting revenue, ordinary purchases or subscriptions for Company products, team subscriptions, usage-based product revenue, pass-through costs, refunds, credits, taxes, payment processing fees, and amounts not payable to the Company, unless the Company expressly approves different treatment in writing for a specific deal.
```

Post-employment payment window:

```text
For agreements signed before employment ends, qualifying commissionable revenue received after employment ends will be paid, if at all, within 90 days after the Company receives cleared funds and completes reconciliation. No commission will be owed on revenue from agreements signed after employment ends unless expressly agreed in writing by the Company.
```

No duplicate commission:

```text
No opportunity or revenue will generate duplicate commission under both a contractor SOW and an employment compensation plan unless the Company expressly agrees otherwise in writing.
```

## Deliverables

Choose the smallest useful deliverable:

- Plan issue summary: highest-risk ambiguities and missing terms.
- Comment pass: margin-comment style notes with suggested language.
- Mechanics checklist: what the plan covers and what it omits.
- Edge-case matrix: scenario, likely outcome under current language, recommended fix.
- Legal-doc architecture memo: where the language should live and what documents must not conflict.
- Redline suggestions: revised clauses or replacement language for specific sections.

## Output Defaults

For a first-pass review, structure the answer as:

1. Executive read: what the plan is trying to do and whether the draft matches it.
2. Biggest risks: ordered by likely business or legal impact.
3. Operational concerns: payout cadence, reconciliation, payroll, and administration.
4. Edge cases: the scenarios most likely to create disputes.
5. Suggested comments or language: concise, practical, and ready to paste.
6. Counsel questions: only the questions that actually need legal review.
