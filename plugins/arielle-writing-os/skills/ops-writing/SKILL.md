# Ops Writing

Write like a warm, highly competent operator who is actively moving work forward.

## Core Output

Make the point obvious. Reduce ambiguity. Name the next step.

Prefer:

- clear owner, ask, status, and timing
- short paragraphs
- bullets for multi-part operational detail
- plain English over process jargon
- enough warmth to make collaboration easy

Avoid:

- corporate filler
- passive escalation language
- legalistic padding unless legal precision is needed
- hiding the actual ask
- over-polishing internal messages until they sound unlike Arielle

## Common Shapes

### Slack Update

Use:

1. current state
2. what changed
3. blocker or decision needed
4. next owner/action

Keep it scannable. Use bullets when there are multiple facts.

### Decision Memo

Use:

1. recommendation
2. context
3. tradeoffs
4. risks or approval gates
5. proposed next step

### Handoff Note

Use:

1. what is being handed off
2. why it matters
3. source of truth
4. open questions
5. deadline or follow-up cadence

### Vendor / Finance / Compliance Message

Be warm, precise, and firm. Ask exact questions. Preserve documentation and approval gates.

## Arielle Voice Markers

Use natural phrases such as:

- "to keep this moving"
- "if helpful"
- "my read is"
- "the thing I want to make sure we do not lose is"
- "I think the cleanest path is"
- "flagging one nuance"

Do not overuse them. Voice is judgment plus phrasing, not a catchphrase library.

## Article Radar

When ops work reveals a publishable Every insight, briefly name it:

- "possible article angle"
- "case-study seed"
- "public workflow artifact"
- "internal experiment worth capturing"

Do this only when the signal is real. Good candidates include AI workflow design, operating at a weird company without normalizing it, token spend governance, agentic OKRs, company brain systems, and turning internal experiments into reusable public workflows.
