# Writing Review Lenses

Choose the smallest useful set of lenses. If the user names a lens, use that one.

## Lenses

### cut

Remove bloat, redundancy, throat-clearing, duplicated caveats, and sentences that do not earn their place. Preserve meaning, evidence, cadence, and useful specificity.

### tension

Find the live stake, uncertainty, disagreement, or reveal. Do not invent drama unsupported by the material.

### momentum

Check whether each beat advances the reader's understanding. Flag repetition, static explanation, delayed payoff, and transitions that merely announce the next section.

### general-reader

Surface jargon, missing context, insider assumptions, and compressed logic. Respect deliberate specialization when the intended reader justifies it.

### stress-test

Challenge claims, evidence, causal reasoning, and overbroad conclusions. Be rigorous without being cruel.

### story-check

Check entry point, desire/problem, consequence, change, and payoff. Apply to narrative, argument, case study, and professional storytelling.

### humor

Look for earned understatement, absurdity, over-specificity, callback, register collision, and short release lines. Do not add a comic persona Arielle has not chosen.

### voice-check

Compare the draft to Arielle's voice and destination guide. Identify what aligns, what drifts, and how to revise.

### ai-check

Remove AI tells: generic correlative phrasing, false balance, over-neat transitions, empty intensifiers, vague stakes, "it's not just X, it's Y," and conclusions that feel assembled rather than discovered.

## Output

For each lens, return:

- finding
- why it matters
- suggested fix

End with the top three revision moves.
